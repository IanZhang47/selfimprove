The supplied file assumed that you read Chinese and English fluently. That has not been established. Select the route below through a brief sample, and revisit it as your skills develop. Chinese-language titles and translators are preserved as options; no language route is compulsory.

## Diagnose support needs

Take a short passage in the language and register you intend to read. Summarize it, explain who is speaking, identify a few important words or images, and compare with a trustworthy annotation or translation. If comprehension requires frequent guessing, add language instruction, glosses, a parallel text, or a translation. This is a placement exercise, not a validated proficiency examination.

| Register | Examples | Possible support |
|---|---|---|
| **Modern Chinese / 现代白话** | 鲁迅 onward and much modern fiction | An appropriate dictionary and notes where vocabulary, dialect, allusion, or historical context require them |
| **Ming–Qing vernacular / 明清白话** | 四大名著, 金瓶梅, 儒林外史 | An annotated edition; vocabulary and idiom may differ from modern usage |
| **Classical Chinese / 文言** | 史记, 诸子, 唐宋古文, classical poetry | Graded instruction, a historical dictionary, notes, and modern-Chinese or other-language translation as needed |

These are useful broad groupings, not three uniform difficulty levels. Works mix registers. Reading modern Chinese does not by itself establish competence in classical Chinese, and knowledge of characters does not eliminate the need to learn Japanese grammar or historical language.

## Building classical Chinese reading ability: candidate resources

1. 王力《古代汉语》(中华书局, four-volume editions): inspect the edition and teaching sequence for a systematic route.
2. 《古文观止》with 中华书局 notes: selected short readings. The collection should not be assumed to be arranged by learner difficulty; grade your selections with guidance.
3. 《古代汉语词典》(商务印书馆): a historical-language dictionary candidate; use a modern 现代汉语词典 for modern language where appropriate.
4. Try a supported pass using 译注, a second pass with notes, and an independent third pass. Check your interpretation afterward. Repeated exposure does not guarantee that a difficult passage has been understood correctly.

## Reading works through more than one language

| Work / source language | Candidate route and decision |
|---|---|
| English originals | Read English when it supports comprehension; use a translation or parallel reading when useful. Shakespeare in 朱生豪 is a translation candidate, not a forbidden route |
| Japanese | For *源氏物语*, compare 林文月 or 丰子恺 in Chinese with Royall Tyler in English if you can use both. Shared characters do not determine fidelity |
| Russian | 草婴 for Tolstoy and 汝龙 for Chekhov remain Chinese candidates; compare the exact work and notes with available English translations |
| French | 傅雷 for Balzac and Romain Rolland remains a Chinese candidate. Examine the translator’s stated approach and a shared passage; neither elegance nor literal syntax alone establishes accuracy |
| Other source languages | Select the actual translator and edition; read the 译序 / 译后记 or equivalent translator’s introduction |

*红楼梦* offers a long-term comparison project between an annotated Chinese edition and Hawkes and Minford’s five-volume *The Story of the Stone*. Examine a small sample of names, poems, dialogue, and allusions. Do not infer that every feature inaccessible in translation is automatically understood by a reader of modern Chinese. Source-language study and translation comparison can continue together.
