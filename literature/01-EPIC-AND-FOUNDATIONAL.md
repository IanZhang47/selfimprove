# Epic and Foundational Narratives

[Literature guide](00-READING-GUIDE.md) · [My study plan](../01-MY-STUDY-PLAN.md) · [Subject map](../03-SUBJECT-MAP.md)

**Catalog status:** Candidate unless a linked source record identifies a specific check. [How to use these labels](00-READING-GUIDE.md#source-status).

Epic, oral, religious, and narrative traditions. These are separate histories with many later interactions; none is a prerequisite for reading all the others. Dates are approximate orientation points, not claims of single authorship or a single recoverable original.

| Work | Region / historical orientation | Candidate editions and scope |
|---|---|---|
| *Epic of Gilgamesh* | Mesopotamia; texts developed over many centuries | Andrew George (Penguin) — scholarly and readable |
| Homer, *Iliad* | Greece, c. 750 BC | Lattimore; Fagles; Wilson (2023). Compare a shared passage and the translators’ stated aims |
| Homer, *Odyssey* | Greece | Fitzgerald or Fagles; Wilson (2017) |
| Aeschylus/Sophocles/Euripides | Greece, 5th c. BC | Grene & Lattimore (Chicago); Fagles for the *Oresteia* |
| Virgil, *Aeneid* | Rome, 19 BC | Ahl (Oxford), Ruden, or Fagles |
| Ovid, *Metamorphoses* | Rome, AD 8 | Lombardo or Raeburn |
| *Mahābhārata* | India | Carole Satyamurti, *Mahabharata: A Modern Retelling* for an adapted narrative; J. A. B. van Buitenen for scholarly translation of books 1–5, not the whole epic. Both scopes source-checked; see [S4](12-SOURCE-REGISTER.md#s4)–[S5](12-SOURCE-REGISTER.md#s5) |
| *Rāmāyaṇa* | India | Goldman and collaborators’ scholarly translation; R. K. Narayan’s short prose retelling. Check the exact volume, completeness, and recension |
| *Bhagavad Gita* | India | Barbara Stoler Miller |
| *Beowulf* | England, c. 8th–11th c. | Heaney; Headley (2020) for a modern jolt |
| Ferdowsi, *Shahnameh* | Persia, c. 1010 | Dick Davis |
| *One Thousand and One Nights* | Arabic, compiled 9th–14th c. | Haddawy; Malcolm and Ursula Lyons. These present different textual selections/traditions; “complete” must refer to a specified source edition, not every tale ever circulated |
| Dante, *Divine Comedy* | Italy, 1320 | Hollander (facing text + notes); Musa; Ciardi |
| *Njál's Saga* & Icelandic sagas | Iceland, 13th c. | Penguin Classics |
| 吴承恩《西游记》 | China, 16th c. | If your reading level supports it: 人民文学出版社 (黄肃秋注本). English route: Anthony C. Yu, revised four-volume *The Journey to the West* ([S7](12-SOURCE-REGISTER.md#s7), source-checked) |
| 罗贯中《三国演义》 | China, 14th c. | Chinese candidate: 人民文学出版社, with suitable notes. Choose a reliable translation if that is more accessible |
| 施耐庵《水浒传》 | China | Chinese candidate: 人民文学出版社 (100回本); check which version and chapter count you want. Translation route: select an explicitly identified source text |
| 紫式部《源氏物语》 | Japan, c. 1010 | Chinese candidates: 林文月 or 丰子恺. English: Royall Tyler ([S3](12-SOURCE-REGISTER.md#s3), source-checked); Arthur Waley is a translation with distinctive choices, not simply “not a translation.” Compare completeness, notes, and style without ranking languages in advance |
| *Popol Vuh* | Maya | Dennis Tedlock |
| *Sunjata* | Mali, oral | Conrad |
