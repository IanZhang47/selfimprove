# Literature Coverage and Discovery

[Literature guide](00-READING-GUIDE.md) · [My study plan](../01-MY-STUDY-PLAN.md) · [Subject map](../03-SUBJECT-MAP.md)

**Catalog status:** Candidate unless a linked source record identifies a specific check. [How to use these labels](00-READING-GUIDE.md#source-status).

The retained catalog is broad but incomplete. It is weighted toward European and East Asian works and toward literature translated into English or Chinese. “All categories” is a continuing coverage task: add forms, languages, communities, periods, and critical methods as gaps become visible. No canonical list stands in for the world’s writing.

| Expansion area | Candidate directions for future source and edition review |
|---|---|
| Southeast Asia | Pramoedya Ananta Toer, *This Earth of Mankind*; Nguyễn Du, *The Tale of Kiều*; José Rizal, *Noli Me Tangere*; regional poetry, drama, and oral traditions |
| Central Asia and the Caucasus | Chingiz Aitmatov, *The Day Lasts More Than a Hundred Years*; Shota Rustaveli, *The Knight in the Panther’s Skin*; regional epics and modern writing |
| Indigenous and Pacific traditions | Leslie Marmon Silko, *Ceremony*; Alexis Wright, *Carpentaria*; Epeli Hauʻofa, *Tales of the Tikongs*; community-authorized oral-literature collections with named tellers, collectors, and translators |
| Caribbean | Jean Rhys, *Wide Sargasso Sea*; Aimé Césaire, *Notebook of a Return to the Native Land*; poetry and prose beyond the already listed Walcott |
| Further African languages, regions, and theater | Mariama Bâ, *So Long a Letter*; Wole Soyinka, *Death and the King’s Horseman*; regional-language works and performance traditions alongside Anglophone and Francophone selections |
| Further Arabic, Palestinian, and Persian writing | Ghassan Kanafani, *Men in the Sun*; Mahmoud Darwish’s poetry; Forugh Farrokhzad’s poetry; compare a named translated collection before selection |
| South Asian languages beyond the current concentration | Ismat Chughtai’s stories; Bama, *Karukku*; regional-language and Dalit literatures, with appropriate historical and linguistic context |
| Speculative, detective, children’s, and young adult writing | Ursula K. Le Guin, *The Left Hand of Darkness*; Octavia E. Butler, *Kindred*; a comparative study of mystery, fantasy, and children’s narrative. Genre is not a disqualification |
| Comics, graphic narrative, and visual storytelling | Art Spiegelman, *Maus*; Marjane Satrapi, *Persepolis*; study image–text relations and assess testimony as nonfiction where relevant |
| Other perspectives and forms | Disability writing, queer literature, working-class writing, migration, oral history, life writing, digital literature, song, screenwriting, and performance; identify specific communities and avoid treating one author as their representative |
| Contemporary work | Add recent works on literary and intellectual grounds; waiting for “durability” would exclude living conversations by design |

These additions are Candidate entries, not a newly verified canon. For each new route, choose a regional or form-specific scholarly introduction, a primary work, and a second voice or contrasting perspective. Record what the selection covers and what it leaves out. Revisiting omissions is part of the lifetime curriculum, not a reason to pretend the initial catalog is complete.

## Discovery lists

The original mentioned thegreatestbooks.org, the Bokklubben World Library, Modern Library 100, Le Monde 100, Guardian 100, and Time 100. Retain them only as optional discovery sources. Their voters, languages, eligibility rules, and aggregation methods select what becomes visible. The earlier numerical claims and “whole-field consensus” language are removed; no ranking is used to certify quality or authority here.
