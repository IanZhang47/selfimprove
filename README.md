# Your lifetime learning library

**Use this start page and the three files below.** They help you decide what to study, find a suitable book, practise what you learn and track progress over a lifetime.

| File | What it answers |
|---|---|

| [02 · How to Study](02-HOW-TO-STUDY.md) | How do I learn, practise, check and retain it? |
| [03 · Subject Map](03-SUBJECT-MAP.md) | Where are the books and routes for each subject? |

## Begin a study window

1. Open the **Subject Map** and choose one main topic, one field for breadth and one social or practical skill. Adjust that workload to your available time.
2. Select a suitable resource and check its prerequisites. Use the **Checked Starting Library** when you want an entry with an official-source record.
3. Fill in **My Study Plan**: the topic, chapters or activity, an outcome you can demonstrate, and the next review date. Use **How to Study** as your working method.

You can study any field over your lifetime. The small active plan keeps the next step manageable.

## Folder layout

- **Four numbered files at the top:** start, plan, method and subject map.
- **subjects/**: one page per domain, with book options and prerequisite/practice guidance.
- **literature/**: a reading guide, genre catalogs, translation and language guidance, and sources.
- **reference/**: shared standards, checked sources, supporting reading lists and background. The **fields/** subfolder contains one checklist per classification family.

Extract the entire ZIP into one folder and open this file in a Markdown reader. Keep the subfolders together so links work.

## What the labels mean

**Source-checked** means the stated identity, edition, scope or access was checked against an official source. It does not certify every claim. **Candidate** means the resource still needs review for the intended route. The folder uses the checks already completed on 9–11 September 2026; this organization pass adds no new verification.
