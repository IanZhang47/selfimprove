## Choose the work

1. **Technical / methods:** choose one defined topic, such as modeling assumptions, computation theory, or analysis. Keep a solved problem set or reproducible analysis, including corrections.
2. **Broad knowledge:** choose one less-studied field and an introductory resource or chapter sequence. Keep a concise explanation, supported source comparison, or another output suited to the field.
3. **Social / practical:** choose one behavior or task to practise with suitable feedback. Record what you tried, what happened, and the next adjustment.

Use the [checked library](reference/02-CHECKED-LIBRARY.md) and [core reading options](reference/03-CORE-READING-OPTIONS.md) to choose resources. The [background and placement reference](reference/07-BACKGROUND-AND-PLACEMENT.md) retains the recommendations from your earlier plan and helps you decide which chapters to sample first. For literature, use the [reading guide](literature/00-READING-GUIDE.md).

Your reported educational background has not been independently verified here, and current skills have not been assessed. **Choose a starting point from work you can demonstrate**, including independent learning and practical experience.

## First two sessions

**Session 1 — find a starting point.** Try two or three representative tasks from the intended resource or course. Explain concepts, solve problems, compare sources, or attempt an appropriate practical task. Note what you can do independently, what returns after review, and what remains unclear. Use the [placement guidance](reference/07-BACKGROUND-AND-PLACEMENT.md#choose-a-starting-point) if needed.

**Session 2 — test the workload.** Study a small section, complete its relevant activity, and check the result. Include exercises and review when estimating your pace. Then fill in a realistic next action for each active track above.

Select a later chapter when the foundations are secure. Review specific prerequisites when knowledge is uneven. Start with orientation when a topic is unfamiliar.

## Review the window

At the date you set above, compare the work with the demonstration you planned. Decide separately for each track whether to continue, review a prerequisite, deepen the route, or open another topic.

Keep unfinished fields on the [subject map](03-SUBJECT-MAP.md). Change active priorities without treating them as permanent exclusions. Progress is demonstrated understanding and capability; a book count is not a completion standard for a discipline.
