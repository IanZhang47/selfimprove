## Choose a starting point

For a field you want to open, choose two or three representative tasks from a suitable introductory source or course. One session can cover explanation; another can cover problems, a source comparison, or an appropriate practical task. This is a planning exercise, not a validated diagnostic test.

Record three things:

| Result | Planning response |
|---|---|
| I can do this independently and check the result. | Select a later chapter or deeper route if it fits your goal. |
| I can do this after review, but some prerequisites are uneven. | Review the specific prerequisites. |
| I cannot yet explain or complete the task. | Start with orientation or a suitable foundation. |

Check work using provided solutions, credible references and qualified feedback where available. Confidence alone is not an assessment; generated explanations need source checks too. Include activity and feedback from the beginning when practical competence is the goal.

Set a [learning stage](../02-HOW-TO-STUDY.md) for a defined topic, rather than assigning one to an entire discipline from a course title or a completed book.

## Technical directions

These are possible routes, not diagnosed deficits or a mandatory sequence. Choose by the next competence you want to develop.

| Direction | Starting question | Reading option and next step |
|---|---|---|
| Computation theory | Can you work with automata, decidability, reductions and complexity at the intended level? | Sipser, *Introduction to the Theory of Computation* — Candidate. Select chapters using a syllabus and sample problems; check proof and discrete-mathematics readiness. |
| Analysis | Which single-variable and multivariable analysis topics can you prove or apply? | Abbott, *Understanding Analysis*, is a single-variable introduction or review. It does not replace the whole MATH411 multivariable continuation. Compare [UMD's course descriptions](https://academiccatalog.umd.edu/undergraduate/approved-courses/math/) and [Abbott's contents](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4939-2712-8). |
| Proof-oriented linear algebra | Are basic matrices, vector spaces and proof skills secure? | Axler, *Linear Algebra Done Right*, 4th ed. — Source-checked [R05](02-CHECKED-LIBRARY.md#r05). A second-course option. |
| Abstract algebra | Are groups, rings, fields or related applications part of the next route? | Dummit & Foote, *Abstract Algebra* — Candidate. Check proof prerequisites and choose a suitable course sequence. Axler is not a universal prerequisite. |
| Modeling and inference | Can you formulate a model, inspect assumptions, criticize fit and explain uncertainty? | McElreath, *Statistical Rethinking* — Candidate. Pair reading with a reproducible analysis, diagnostics and sensitivity checks. Include other inferential approaches as the question requires. |

Statistical calculation, media literacy, research integrity and study design overlap but are not identical. Previous coursework does not automatically eliminate any of these resources.

For learning design, consult Source-checked [R01: *How People Learn II*](02-CHECKED-LIBRARY.md#r01) and [R29: *How Learning Works*](02-CHECKED-LIBRARY.md#r29). Use an appropriate statistics or causal-inference resource when needed. Study methods can develop alongside subject study; they do not require postponing every other field.

The original personalized estimate of “35 hours instead of 98” was unsupported. The original Phase 0 alone totaled 58 estimated hours; 98 included the separate learning-methods phase. Set a personal estimate only after selecting chapters and exercises and sampling actual pace.
