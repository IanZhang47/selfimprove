Use this register when a book marked **†** in a subject file raises a question, or when you want to check a specific claim. The **31 original review rows** below are questions to investigate, not findings that the books are false. A second table preserves the core reading plan's 11 additional prompts; some overlap.

Before recording a factual criticism, attach the exact passage or claim, suitable evidence and date, and any substantive response. Distinguish a book's argument from the evidence for its empirical claims. The general assessment process is in [Source Standards](01-SOURCE-STANDARDS.md).

## Questions from the subject catalog

| Original book/author entry | Verification question; unresolved unless independently sourced |
|---|---|
| Harari, *Sapiens* | Which specific historical, anthropological or biological claims are being used, and how do specialist sources support or challenge each one? |
| Gladwell, *Outliers* | Does the text distinguish hours of practice from practice quality, starting conditions, domain differences and selection effects? |
| Duckworth, *Grit* | How is grit measured, how does it differ from conscientiousness, and what incremental prediction or intervention evidence supports the claim? |
| Baumeister, *Willpower* | Which proposed self-control or ego-depletion effect is at issue, and what do suitable replications and current syntheses find? |
| Cuddy, *Presence* | Separate subjective confidence from hormonal, behavioural and performance outcomes; what evidence supports each claim? |
| Fredrickson, *Positivity* | What mathematical model and evidence support any numerical positivity threshold, and are there corrections or retractions for the cited work? |
| Kahneman, *Thinking, Fast and Slow* | Which claims are being used? Check the cited studies and later evidence, especially before generalizing priming effects; do not grade every chapter as one claim. |
| Bargh, *Before You Know It* | Which priming effect, measurement and setting are involved, and what do preregistered replications and meta-analyses show? |
| Diamond, *Guns, Germs, and Steel* | What are the proposed causal mechanisms and geographical scope? Compare specialist histories and evidence for institutions, contingency and human agency. |
| Graeber & Wengrow, *The Dawn of Everything* | Identify the archaeological claim, site, date and source; compare relevant specialist interpretations and the authors' response. |
| Acemoglu & Robinson, *Why Nations Fail* | Distinguish the institutional hypothesis from particular historical cases, causal identification and competing explanations. |
| Piketty, *Capital* | Specify the edition, dataset, wealth/income concept and causal proposition; inspect data, corrections and alternative explanations separately. |
| Hattie, *Visible Learning* | How were studies selected and effect sizes combined? Check comparability, quality, dependence and whether rankings support the classroom decision. |
| Walker, *Why We Sleep* | Trace the specific sleep statistic, mechanism or health claim to its source and compare current specialist references; do not treat a trade account as an advanced textbook. |
| van der Kolk, *The Body Keeps the Score* | Separate descriptions of trauma, proposed mechanisms and treatment claims; check the evidence for the particular population and outcome. |
| Barrett, *How Emotions Are Made* | Which claims are findings and which depend on a theory of emotion? Compare competing theories and evidence without implying the field has no established findings. |
| Clear, *Atomic Habits* / Duhigg, *Power of Habit* | Separate a practical habit procedure from the biological or psychological explanation offered for it; define what outcome would justify keeping the procedure. |
| Pink, *Drive* | Check when motivation claims apply, which outcomes were measured and what the underlying self-determination research actually supports. |
| Newport, *Deep Work* | Distinguish a useful work routine from empirical claims about attention, productivity and universal optimum schedules. |
| Gottman, *Seven Principles* | If a prediction-accuracy number is used, inspect the sample, base rate, model construction and genuinely out-of-sample validation; evaluate practical advice separately. |
| Cialdini, *Influence* | Inspect the particular persuasion principle, study and current evidence, including boundary conditions and cultural context. |
| Christensen, *The Innovator's Dilemma* | Define disruption precisely; check prospective predictions, case selection, failures and competing business explanations. |
| Pollan, *The Omnivore's Dilemma* | Separate food reporting and personal values from nutritional and agronomic claims; verify the specific scientific proposition. |
| McLuhan, *Understanding Media* | Which passages are metaphor or interpretive theory, and which make empirical predictions that could be investigated? |
| Zuboff, *Surveillance Capitalism* | Identify the specific documented platform practice, measurement or economic claim; distinguish it from political and ethical argument. |
| Ahrens, *How to Take Smart Notes* | Does comparative evidence show this note system improves the outcome you need relative to simpler alternatives? Evaluate the time cost and your own retrieval/use. |
| Martin, *Clean Code* | Treat rules as context-sensitive design proposals; compare alternatives on readability, maintainability, correctness and the actual codebase. |
| Goodfellow et al., *Deep Learning* | Which mathematical foundations remain relevant, and which architectures/training practices need newer sources? Publication date alone does not invalidate stable material. |
| Campbell, *Hero with a Thousand Faces* / Frazer, *The Golden Bough* | Examine selection of traditions, translations and counterexamples; distinguish historical influence from current endorsement of a universal explanatory scheme. |
| Wyner, *Fluent Forever* | Separate useful language exercises from claims about memory or neuroscience; verify prerequisites, feedback and performance in the target language. |
| Kiyosaki, Hill, Byrne, Greene | The original supplied authors without specific books. Identify the exact title and claim before judging it; distinguish anecdote, argument, instruction and empirical evidence. |

## Additional claim prompts from the core reading plan

**Status: Candidate / claim-level assessment pending.** These prompts are retained unchanged; they add specific titles and alternative questions without adding new judgments.

| Resource | What to examine before relying on a specific claim |
|---|---|
| Sapiens | Which claims are established findings, broad historical interpretations, or speculative reconstructions? |
| Outliers | Does an expertise claim generalize across domains, and what conditions does the underlying research impose? |
| Grit | How is the construct measured, distinguished from related traits, and connected to outcomes? |
| Willpower | Which claims about depletion, self-control or interventions remain supported by stronger subsequent studies? |
| Presence | Separate self-reported experience, physiological claims and behavioral outcomes; inspect their evidence independently. |
| Positivity | Check the exact numerical-ratio claim, relevant mathematical criticism, and the publication record before describing any correction. |
| Blink; The Power of Habit; Emotional Intelligence | Trace each important mechanism or practical claim to its research rather than judging all three books together. |
| Thinking, Fast and Slow | Assess individual claims and chapters against subsequent evidence; do not treat criticism of one area as a verdict on the whole book. |
| Guns, Germs, and Steel | Compare causal historical interpretations and their sources with serious regional scholarship. |
| The Body Keeps the Score | Separate memoir/clinical narrative, mechanistic claims and treatment-effect claims; use suitable current evidence for clinical questions. |
| Rich Dad Poor Dad; Think and Grow Rich; The Secret; 48 Laws of Power | Distinguish anecdotes, values and proposed strategies from reproducible evidence and current financial or behavioral knowledge. |

## Correction notes retained from the revised catalog

- Ross's *A First Course in Probability* is an undergraduate alternative, not an automatic graduate next rung: [MIT 18.440 assigned it](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-440-probability-and-random-variables-spring-2014/pages/readings/). It is retained in the probability core.
- Culler's *Literary Theory: A VSI* is moved to orientation; Eagleton's introduction remains a further introduction/core option. Walker's *Why We Sleep* remains a trade perspective with specific claims to check; a clinical sleep reference is not a beginner's stepping stone.
- The *Behaviour Change Wheel* book is by Michie, **Atkins** and West according to [UCL's book record](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/brain-sciences/behaviour-change/resources/cbc-bookshop-associated-publications). Michie, **van Stralen** and West authored the [2011 research paper](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1305716/), retained as a distinct related source. Do not merge their authorship.
- The physics books are candidate teaching choices. Agreement between self-study guides does not establish field-wide consensus or a uniquely correct sequence.
- [Bishop and Bishop's official site](https://www.bishopbook.com/) distinguishes free online reading of *Deep Learning: Foundations and Concepts* from its ebook purchase. Access is resource- and format-specific. See [Access and Formats](06-ACCESS-AND-FORMATS.md) and the [checked library](02-CHECKED-LIBRARY.md) before obtaining any title.
- Scope/edition reminders: *Linear Algebra Done Right* is a proof-oriented second linear-algebra course in [R05](02-CHECKED-LIBRARY.md#r05); *How Learning Works* [R29](02-CHECKED-LIBRARY.md#r29) identifies the second edition and its authors; *Gordis Epidemiology* [R45](02-CHECKED-LIBRARY.md#r45) identifies the current checked title and authors. The short labels in tables are catalog pointers, not exact citations for every edition.
- The earlier “~190 subjects” count was incorrect: **241 subject rows** appear in the original domain tables. This edition adds **40 route-extension records**; overlapping topics mean these should not be treated as 281 mutually exclusive academic disciplines. The earlier “~440 books” was not an audited unique-title count, and generic series/author entries cannot be counted as single books. Even if using 440 as an estimate, 440/2,500 is **17.6%**, not 10%; neither fraction measures a share of all knowledge.
