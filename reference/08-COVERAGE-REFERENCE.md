Use the [Subject Map](../03-SUBJECT-MAP.md) to choose reading. This reference shows how the earlier 25-area overview relates to the official field classification. The different groupings serve navigation and traceability; they are not additional degrees to complete.

The [NCES Classification of Instructional Programs](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/cipcode/browse.aspx?y=56) provides a checkable inventory of fields rather than relying on my memory of university majors. The retrieved version contains 50 top-level headers, including two reserved placeholders, 464 branch entries, including seven reserved placeholders, and 2,173 detailed program entries, including 30 reserved placeholders. The 48 nonreserved families, 457 nonreserved branches, and 2,143 nonreserved detailed entries are a coverage index, not that many independent degrees: some are general, overlapping, residual, nondegree, or professional training categories.

Use [UNESCO's international field descriptions](https://www.uis.unesco.org/sites/default/files/medias/fichiers/2025/04/international-standard-classification-of-education-fields-of-education-and-training-2013-detailed-field-descriptions-2015-en.pdf) as a second perspective. Neither taxonomy includes every emerging specialization or every form of human knowledge. Inclusion in a classification also does not establish that a practice is effective or a theory true.

Within every relevant area, check geographical and cultural scope. An explicitly Western music history is a useful regional study, not the world's music history. A Chinese history text does not cover Japan, Korea, Southeast Asia, or Central Asia. Read serious scholarship from the traditions being studied and identify the period, region, language, and perspective represented.
