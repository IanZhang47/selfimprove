Official-source checks: 9–11 September 2026. This reorganized copy preserves those checks; it adds no new verification.

**55 entries across the major study areas.** Some entries are multi-volume sequences or living references. This is a starting library, not 55 books that collectively contain all knowledge, and not a complete bibliography for every major in the [field index](fields/00-INDEX.md).

**What was checked:** title, authorship or responsible organization, edition where specified, intended subject coverage, and access where described. Official author, publisher, university, professional-body, or government links are provided. Publisher descriptions establish intended scope; they do not prove the accuracy of every statement or the effectiveness of every technique. Curricular placement below is my recommendation.

**What qualifies a resource here:** identifiable relevant expertise, a defined teaching/reference purpose, and a route to inspect the source. For several open textbooks, the official site also provides exercises, revision/errata information, and teaching materials. These are stronger starting points than a popularity ranking. Before committing to a specialist route, additionally examine sample content, prerequisites, independent academic use, corrections, and substantive critiques.

Level labels: **Intro** = orientation or introductory course; **Core** = sustained foundational study; **Advanced** = assumes prior study; **Reference** = consult as needed; **Practice** = skills instruction requiring activity and feedback. A resource may serve more than one role.

## How to build a complete route beyond these anchors

Choose the specific branch in the field index. Compare suitable current university or professional curricula. List prerequisites and central competencies, then choose the smallest set of texts that covers them with sufficient depth and usable assessment. Add laboratory, studio, field, language, social or supervised professional work as required by the actual competence.

Evaluate textbook alternatives on clarity, coverage, exercises and feedback, transparent evidence, corrections and suitability for self-study. A more difficult or more famous book is not automatically better. An older text can remain valuable for stable foundations while being unsuitable for current clinical rules, technology, standards or data.

Keep a record of sections read, work completed, misconceptions corrected, and what remains uncertain. Expand the map as new disciplines and better resources appear. The initial 55 entries make a broad foundation available; specialist routes throughout the thousands of indexed program categories remain explicitly open rather than falsely labeled verified.
